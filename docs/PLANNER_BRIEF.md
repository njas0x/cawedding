# Wedding Planning Brief: Authentic Chinese Banquet

## The Vision

An 80s/90s-style Chinese restaurant wedding, replicating exactly how they were done for immigrant families back then—think bustling Cantonese banquet halls in US Chinatowns or suburbs like Monterey Park or Irvine, where the focus was on family, food, and tradition without any frills or staging. We're drawing from the era's authentic traditions brought over by Hong Kong, Taiwanese, and Cantonese immigrants, with slight modern comfort updates like a lounge area for second-half mingling to allow guests to relax post-dinner. Imagine a lively evening where elders reminisce over symbolic dishes, kids run around, and everyone leaves with full bellies and red envelopes exchanged—no Instagram aesthetics, just genuine cultural warmth.

## Core Principles

- **Authentic, not stylized** - This is how our parents' generation did it: practical, family-driven, and rooted in Cantonese customs.
- **Restaurant-run** - They have the system (from setup to service), we use it without interference.
- **Food-centric** - The banquet IS the event; everything revolves around the multi-course meal and shared toasts.
- **Natural flow** - No forced moments or rigid timeline; let conversations and courses unfold organically.

## The Venue

A traditional Chinese seafood restaurant in Orange County specializing in Cantonese banquets, with:

- Main dining hall for 200-220 guests (20-22 round tables of 10, to encourage family-style sharing).
- Small stage for tea ceremony and speeches (simple setup with microphone and basic backdrop).
- Secondary lounge/arcade space (for casual mingling, perhaps with a few retro games or mahjong tables as a nod to family pastimes, but keeping it understated).
- Existing décor (red curtains, gold accents, 囍 signs, glossy tiled floors, warm incandescent lighting—nothing added or altered).
- Standard wedding package they've done hundreds of times, ideally in a location like Irvine or Westminster for easy access.

## The Program

This is a loose guide—adjust based on the restaurant's natural pacing. Courses will be served family-style throughout the evening, starting after the grand entrance and continuing at the venue's rhythm.

- **6:00pm** Doors open, guests arrive (pre-dinner drinks and appetizers available; red envelope station setup for gifts).
- **6:40pm** Grand entrance (bride and groom enter to applause, perhaps with simple door games if family insists, but keep light).
- **6:55pm** Tea ceremony on stage (parents/elders only; bride and groom serve tea for blessings, with red envelopes given in return).
- **7:35pm** Toasts (one from family, one from a friend—keep short and heartfelt!).
- **7:45pm** LOUNGE OPENS - guests can flow between spaces for mingling, snacks, or light games.
- **8:15pm** Cake/champagne moment (simple cutting and toasts; no elaborate tiered cake unless venue standard).
- **8:45pm** Group photo on stage (quick and inclusive for all guests).
- **9:00pm** Karaoke begins, free mingling (venue-provided system with classic Cantonese songs from the 80s/90s).
- **9:45pm** Final thanks (from couple and families).
- **10:00pm** Service ends (guests depart naturally; no formal send-off).

## Atmosphere and Guest Experience

To help you visualize: The hall buzzes with multi-generational chatter—elders in traditional attire sharing stories in Cantonese, younger guests mixing English conversations, clinking glasses over Tsingtao beer or chrysanthemum tea. Smells of steaming dim sum, roasted suckling pig, and stir-fried lobster fill the air. Lighting is warm and practical (incandescent bulbs with some fluorescents), creating a cozy, lived-in feel like a big family reunion. Guests (a mix of family from Asia/US, friends, and colleagues) wear semi-formal outfits—women in qipao or dresses, men in suits or button-ups. The vibe is glamorous in its simplicity: joyful, noisy, and heartfelt, with kids playing and natural laughter, evoking memories of 80s/90s immigrant weddings where community trumped perfection.

## Specific Requirements

### Must Have:

- Tea ceremony setup on stage (trays, teacups, cushions for elders—symbolizing respect and blessings).
- 8-10 course Cantonese banquet (symbolic dishes like whole suckling pig for prosperity, lobster or shrimp for happiness, abalone for wealth, steamed fish for abundance, roasted chicken for harmony, longevity noodles, and sweet desserts; family-style service).
- Red envelope (hongbao) collection system (secure box or table for monetary gifts, with thank-you notes).
- Bilingual announcements (Cantonese/English to include all guests).
- Natural documentation photography (focus on authentic captures: toasts, tea pouring, family interactions).

### Nice to Have:

- Karaoke system (venue has; load with 80s/90s hits like Teresa Teng songs).
- Simple photo corner (just a plain backdrop with 囍 symbol for quick snaps).
- Minimal flowers for head table (fresh chrysanthemums or carnations in red/gold tones, sourced affordably).
