# Wedding Planner Call Interview Questions

## Opening (2 minutes)
"Thanks for taking the time to chat! We're planning a January 2026 wedding at a Chinese restaurant in Orange County. Before we dive in, could you give me a quick overview of your experience with Asian or multicultural weddings?"

---

## Section 1: Cultural Experience (5 minutes)

### Must-Ask Questions:

1. **"Have you coordinated weddings at Chinese restaurants like Capital Seafood, Sea Harbour, or similar venues?"**
   - Listen for: Specific venue names, understanding of how restaurants operate
   - Follow-up: "What's different about working with restaurant venues?"

2. **"Can you walk me through a tea ceremony you've coordinated?"**
   - Listen for: Proper sequence (serve elders first), red envelope process, kneeling cushions
   - Follow-up: "How do you handle the bilingual aspects?"

3. **"What's your experience with Chinese wedding traditions beyond the tea ceremony?"**
   - Listen for: Door games, hair combing, multiple outfit changes, lucky colors
   - Follow-up: "How do you balance tradition with modern preferences?"

### Deeper Probes (if time):
- "Have you worked with bilingual MCs before?"
- "How familiar are you with Chinese banquet service flow?"
- "What Chinese or Asian vendors do you typically work with?"

---

## Section 2: Logistics & Timeline (5 minutes)

### Must-Ask Questions:

4. **"Our timeline is tight - 3-4 months from September to January. How would you approach this?"**
   - Listen for: Confidence, systems, priority order
   - Red flag: "That's impossible" or major concerns

5. **"The restaurant handles food and basic venue setup. What would you handle?"**
   - Listen for: Clear understanding of scope
   - Should mention: Vendors, decor additions, timeline, ceremonies, coordination

6. **"How do you work WITH a restaurant's existing setup rather than transforming it?"**
   - Listen for: Respect for venue, minimal intervention approach
   - Red flag: Wants to bring in extensive decor or cover everything

### Deeper Probes (if time):
- "What's your typical planning process for short timelines?"
- "How many weddings do you have between now and January?"
- "What vendors would you recommend we book first?"

---

## Section 3: Vision & Style (3 minutes)

### Must-Ask Questions:

7. **"We want authentic Chinese banquet feel with subtle modern touches - think soft neon accents and city pop music. How would you approach this?"**
   - Listen for: Understanding of 'subtle', respect for authentic base
   - Red flag: Suggests major transformations or doesn't get the balance

8. **"How would you add personality without over-decorating the space?"**
   - Listen for: Lighting, small details, music, guest experience focus
   - Good answers: Candles, simple neon signs, playlist curation

### Deeper Probes (if time):
- "What's your philosophy on wedding design?"
- "Can you share an example where you enhanced rather than transformed a venue?"

---

## Section 4: Practical Details (3 minutes)

### Must-Ask Questions:

9. **"What's included in your coordination package and what's your fee?"**
   - Listen for: Clear pricing, what's included/excluded
   - Confirm: Within $3,000-5,000 range

10. **"How do you handle communication leading up to and during the wedding?"**
    - Listen for: Response time expectations, emergency protocols
    - Important: Day-of team size and language capabilities

### Deeper Probes (if time):
- "Do you have insurance?"
- "What's your payment schedule?"
- "What happens if you're sick on the wedding day?"

---

## Section 5: References & Next Steps (2 minutes)

### Must-Ask Questions:

11. **"Can you provide 2-3 references from similar weddings?"**
    - Listen for: Willingness, specific examples
    - Note: If hesitant, that's a red flag

12. **"What questions do you have for us?"**
    - Good signs: Asks about family dynamics, specific traditions, guest demographics
    - Red flags: Only asks about budget or seems disinterested

### Closing:
"This has been really helpful! We're talking to a few planners this week and plan to decide by [date]. When would you need to know to hold our date?"

---

## Situational Questions (Use 2-3 if time permits)

**Scenario 1: Timeline Crunch**
"The photographer we want is only available if we move the tea ceremony 30 minutes earlier. The restaurant says that would conflict with their service. How would you handle this?"
- Looking for: Problem-solving, negotiation skills, creative solutions

**Scenario 2: Family Dynamics**
"Grandma speaks only Cantonese and wants to give a blessing during the tea ceremony. How would you coordinate this?"
- Looking for: Cultural sensitivity, bilingual solutions

**Scenario 3: Last-Minute Issue**
"Day-of: The neon sign vendor is running 2 hours late. What do you do?"
- Looking for: Backup plans, calm under pressure

**Scenario 4: Budget Reality**
"We love a photographer but they're $500 over budget. How would you advise us?"
- Looking for: Creative solutions, vendor negotiation experience

---

## Questions to Answer for Them

Be prepared to answer:
- Approximate guest count (200-220)
- Venue type (Chinese restaurant, likely Capital Seafood)
- Must-haves (tea ceremony, bilingual MC, minimal decor)
- Nice-to-haves (neon accents, city pop playlist, photo booth)
- Deal-breakers (over-transformation, unavailable for timeline)

## Quick Phone Screen Version (5 minutes)

If you need to quickly screen before a longer call:

1. "Are you available for a January 2026 wedding with September start?"
2. "Have you done Chinese restaurant weddings before?"
3. "Is your full coordination package within $3,000-5,000?"
4. "Do you have experience with tea ceremonies?"
5. "Can we schedule a longer call this week?"

---

## Red Flags During Call

STOP the call politely if they:
- Immediately say the timeline is impossible
- Have no Asian wedding experience and seem dismissive
- Quote way over budget ($7,000+)
- Are clearly unavailable for your dates
- Seem confused about restaurant venues

Polite exit: "I really appreciate your time! This doesn't sound like quite the right fit for our specific needs, but I wish you all the best with your other weddings."
